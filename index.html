The idea

A wristband that plays "hot and cold" with your buddy.

Buddy separation is the most common start of a diving incident. Today's answer is banging a tank and hoping. The one electronic product in this space can shout "come here" — but it can't tell you how far away your buddy is.

The one-liner: the competitor tells your buddy you want them. DBA tells you where they are.

Buddy-Watcher (today)

  • Dive-computer-sized unit, buckle strap, two sizes
  • Press → buddy buzzes. That's it.
  • USB charging port
  • ≈ $220 per pair

DBA (this concept)

  • Slip-on silicone band, 38 × 28 × 11.5 mm module
  • Call and distance-coded vibration
  • Fully sealed, charges inductively
  • Target $179–199 per pair · ≈ $86 to build

DAN Europe's reviewers of the incumbent asked for exactly one thing it lacked: distance to buddy.

How it works · try it

Drag the second diver. Feel the band find them.

Radio dies within centimetres of seawater, so the band uses sound — a tiny sonar handshake. Band A pings at 65 kHz, band B answers, and the round-trip time gives the distance (sound moves 1.5 km every second in water, so 20 m apart is a 27 ms echo). The band turns that number into vibration cadence.

What's honest about this

One wrist transducer gives distance, not direction. You move, and the pulses speed up or slow down — exactly the "hot and cold" the brief describes. Direction-finding needs an array and is a Rev B question.
The 20 m range and the cost figures are physics-and-comparables estimates. Phase 1 — two breadboards in a pool, ~$5–10k — exists to prove them before anyone pays for moulds.
